# Goals

Pulled directly from [[My Identity]] — its own "Current Major Arcs," "Career Direction," and "Contradictions" sections. Not inferred from activity; only what's explicitly stated there.

## Current

*(My Identity frames these as "Current Major Arcs" — kept under Current for that reason.)*

- Become a genuinely strong software engineer — "someone who understands systems," not "student who knows frameworks."
- Get very good at applied AI engineering — especially agents, reliability, evaluation, observability, security.
- Build products people actually use — move beyond hackathon demos toward users, feedback, revenue.
- Develop broad world knowledge — a connected understanding of technology, economics, culture, systems.
- Become financially independent — build useful skills, increase earning power, understand money rather than merely earning it.
- Teach and create — share what's understood, eventually produce things with a longer lifespan than another GitHub repo.
- Write — keep writing part of life, including working on a book, not limiting output to code and Discord messages.

## Long Term

- "Become the kind of engineer who can be dropped into an unfamiliar system, understand it quickly, find important problems, and ship reliable solutions" — stated explicitly as the goal, in contrast to "just get a software job."
- Drawn toward environments where: the team is small enough that ownership matters, working alongside strong engineers, things move quickly, work reaches real users, consequences of engineering decisions are visible, and increasingly difficult problems are trusted to you.

## Things Worth Reconsidering

*(My Identity's own "Contradictions" and "Things I Need To Watch" sections — flagged there as tensions to manage, not resolved conclusions.)*

- Curiosity becoming fragmentation — "being interested in everything can easily become becoming excellent at nothing."
- Shipping becoming activity — PR count, commits, and hours are proxies; the real question is "what became better because I worked on it."
- AI replacing struggle — "if AI solves every difficult thought immediately, I lose something important."
- Overengineering — seeing failure modes is useful, but "can also lead to fixing hypothetical problems that don't justify the complexity."
- Chasing opportunities — "more opportunities are not automatically better. Compounding in a good environment can beat constantly restarting somewhere new."
