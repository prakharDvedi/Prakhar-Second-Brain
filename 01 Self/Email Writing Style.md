# Email Writing Style

Voice for emails I write to people outside my own team, like company outreach and recruiter messages. Built from [[My Writing Style]]: direct, simple and natural, but a bit more polished than chat. Keeps the casual feel without sounding like a template.

## Core

Direct and to the point. Say the reason for writing in the first or second sentence.

Simple words over formal ones. Use "share", "reach out", "fit" over "leverage", "facilitate", "synergy".

Short paragraphs, usually 2 to 4 sentences.

Sounds like a person who knows what they are asking, not a form letter.

## Capitalization and punctuation

Normal sentence case. Proper capitalization for names, companies and technical terms (Flutter, Kafka, IIIT Bhopal). Not lowercase, unlike chat.

Sparse punctuation. Periods and commas. No semicolons. No em dashes. No exclamation marks.

## Tone

Friendly, not eager. No fake enthusiasm and no motivational lines.

Firm about the fit, light about the ask. "I'm sharing these profiles for your consideration" works. "Would you be open to considering these profiles?" does not.

Confident about the candidates. Honest about what I do not know. Never imply a vacancy is confirmed unless the source shows one.

## Current job search

I am looking for entry-level internship roles in software engineering, and the students I share are available for internships. Say this softly in outreach emails, with a varied wording each time. Do not imply that a specific opening exists unless the source shows one.

## Voice in outreach

Only the introduction uses "I" (who I am and why I'm writing). Every other sentence uses "we" or "our", for example "we're exploring", "our batch", "let us know". Do not say "a few" when the table lists all the candidates.

## Phrases I use

"Hope you're doing well."

"Please let me know if I can share any other details."

"Best regards," then name and "B.Tech, IIIT Bhopal".

## Avoid

"I'd be happy to", "Furthermore", "Moreover", "It is worth noting that", "leverage", "synergy".

Overly polished closers and stacked adjectives.

Placement-office or official-representative language. Introduce myself as Prakhar from IIIT Bhopal, nothing more.

Invented stats, outcomes or hiring claims.

Typos or casing tricks used to look casual.
